You go in two hours before the doors open to the public, in a group of ten, with no other groups in the building. The Vatican Museums take more than 25,000 visitors a day; this is what they look like when they are empty.

The marble halls and frescoed galleries of the Apostolic Palaces come first — the Apollo Belvedere, the Laocoön and the Belvedere Torso in the Clementine Courtyard, Nero's monumental bathtub, the galleries of Candelabra, Tapestries and 17th-century maps. Then Julius II's private apartments, painted by Raphael, with the School of Athens to yourself.

Then the Sistine Chapel, before anyone else is in it. Michelangelo's Creation of Man and Last Judgment, in a room that is normally shoulder to shoulder.

Afterwards your guide walks you back to the Pinecone Courtyard for breakfast. An evening option runs after closing instead: no breakfast, and about twenty minutes shorter. Everyone needs a government-issued ID to enter, whatever their age, and backpacks are not allowed inside.
