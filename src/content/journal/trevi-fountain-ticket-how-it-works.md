Do you need a ticket for the Trevi Fountain? Not to see it. The fountain can be seen for free from the piazza at any hour, and since 2 February 2026 the €2 ticket is needed only if you do not live in Rome and want to enter the inner area by the basin, from 9.00 to 22.00, or from 11.30 on Mondays and Fridays. There is no time slot to book: you can buy the ticket online, at the Civic Museums and Tourist Infopoints, or at the entrance on Via della Stamperia, where only cards are accepted.

The city's notice gives the scheme a run from 30 January 2026 to 31 December 2027. Every hour, price and rule below comes from the operator's site, fontanaditrevi.roma.it, and from the city's tourism site, turismoroma.it, both read in November 2026. Times can change for maintenance or public order, so look at the official page the week you go. We have filmed the fountain in eight different months of the year, in winter and in summer.

## What the €2 ticket covers, and what stays free

The Trevi Fountain ticket gives access to the inner perimeter of the fountain, which the city describes as the basin, the part closest to the monument. The operator's site says the ticket helps protect the fountain and ensure better maintenance.

Everything else stays free. The fountain is always visible from outside, without a ticket, and the fee applies only to the area by the water. There are steps to reach that area. If you have a pushchair, you can leave it for a while at the access control point, but nobody looks after it.

## Trevi Fountain opening hours and last entry

The ticket applies at these times:

- Monday and Friday: 11.30 to 22.00
- Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday: 9.00 to 22.00

Last entry is at 21.00 every day. The city's notice adds that the fee also applies on the first Sunday of the month.

A few days in November and December start at a different time. Entry begins at 14.00 on Monday 23 November and on Mondays 7 and 21 December, at 11.30 on Thursday 24 and Tuesday 29 December, and at 9.00 on Friday 25 and Monday 28 December.

## Who enters the Trevi Fountain for free

According to the operator's site, these visitors do not pay:

- residents of Rome or the Metropolitan City, showing an ID card at the access control
- children under 6
- people with disabilities, and one person accompanying them
- licensed tour guides, showing a valid badge
- holders of a valid Roma MIC Card

Bring the document that proves you qualify and show it at the ticket office. If you cannot, you buy a ticket like everyone else.

## Where to buy Trevi Fountain tickets, and how to pay

Online, the official shop is [fontanaditrevi.vivaticket.it](https://fontanaditrevi.vivaticket.it/), linked from the operator's site. You can also buy at the Civic Museums, the Tourist Infopoints and authorised retailers, paying in cash or by card. At the fountain itself only cards are accepted.

A ticket bought in advance has no date or time, so you can use it on any day. It cannot be refunded or passed to someone else. Anyone who already has a ticket, or enters free, can go straight to the dedicated line; everyone else queues at the ticket office to buy one.

The ticket office and the entrance are on Via della Stamperia. You leave on the other side, through the side gate on Via dei Crociferi. The nearest metro stop is Barberini, on line A, about five minutes away on foot.

## The rules by the basin, and throwing a coin

You can still throw a coin. The rules forbid pouring liquids or throwing any object into the fountain, "except for the traditional coin toss".

Inside the area you may not eat or drink, smoke or sit on the edge of the basin. Sitting, loitering, eating, drinking or climbing can bring a fine of €40 to €240. Wading into the water or taking water from the fountain brings a fine starting at €450. Damaging or defacing the fountain, its base or the area around it can mean up to a year in prison or a fine of at least €2,065.

There are no toilets and no cloakroom, so carry a small bag. Dogs must be in a carrier, or on a lead and muzzled. Bicycles are not allowed in.

## Best time to visit the Trevi Fountain

The ticket applies only during the hours above. Outside them, the operator's site says the fountain remains visible for free. It does not say whether the area by the basin stays open, so early in the morning or after 22.00 plan to see it from the piazza.

If you want to stand by the water, go during the ticketed hours and arrive before the last entry at 21.00. The operator's home page shows a crowd level indicator, based on average attendance trends, which is worth a look before you set off.

## Good to know

### Is the Trevi Fountain free?

Yes, to look at from the piazza, at any hour. The €2 ticket is for visitors from outside Rome who want to enter the area by the basin during the ticketed hours.

### Do I need to book a time slot?

No. Tickets bought in advance carry no date or time, and you can also buy one at the entrance by card.

### Can I pay cash at the Trevi Fountain?

Not at the entrance, which takes cards only. Cash is accepted at the Civic Museums, the Tourist Infopoints and authorised retailers.

### How long will the €2 fee last?

The city's notice sets the scheme from 30 January 2026 to 31 December 2027, with the fee charged from 2 February 2026.
